FireFlies.ai Design Task

by Shater Tsavsar2026

I’ve been tasked with creating a mobile first catch-up app. The idea is to have a central hub for all your context that you can access immediately and at a glance know what to do next without overwhelming the user.

Key needs from our users,

  • Know fast if anything needs her, and by when.
  • Trust what she’s told.
  • Act in one tap.

Assumptions,

  • Fireflies is connected to calendar, Slack and email, and can post for her after a confirmation.
  • User has under 90 seconds and one hand.
  • A normal return has two to five real asks.

Deciding what should be surfaced is dependent on priority and also the user’s role on the team as this is a context solution. Of course want the user to know where all these details are coming from, but if we have 20 slack messages, we can’t throw this in the user’s face because that would defeat the “Without overwhelming the user” part we mentioned above so grouping and then generating a summary but we can’t just group slack notifications, that would be a “Slack round-up”, so how do we handle contextual grouping?

Say we’re working with an engineer, we can take a conversation about a certain feature, conversations that happened over email and over slack and then the repo activity on Github, now we’ve created an overview on all that activity by cross referencing from 3 platforms.

Side note:

I’m building an opensource email client and this is on of our features so I had done a bunch of the research and ideating already, would love to chat more about that later.

Approach and key decisions

One hand, 30 to 90 seconds.

The goal of the design was to give the user their content and let them navigate the entire app productively with one hand, because this is a 30 to 90 second window of use.

Adding native iOS behaviour like the swipe above was a quick and easy way to get there. Something small with a large impact is using the logos to reinforce trust in where things are coming from and where they are going, instead of relying on “they should know”.

In so many places there are keys telling the user exactly what is going to happen, like letting them know that yes, this is going to this specific Slack thread. The one-handed feel holds because the AI does most of the typing, with replies drafted for the context they are in.

Then there is Ask, which sits where the thumb already is, with suggested questions so most of the time there is nothing to type.

There is quick access to calls too, so the user can get a quick prep in, or even join the call on the go and switch devices once they are settled.

Again, we are imagining this person walking out of a conference room holding files or a laptop, trying to figure out “OK, what is next, or what did I miss?” while also getting to the next one. We make sure they don’t miss any unknowns.
