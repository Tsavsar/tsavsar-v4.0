Fireflies.ai Design Task

by Shater Tsavsar2026

I was tasked with designing a mobile-first catch-up app. The idea is a central hub for all your context, one you can open and, at a glance, know what to do next, without being overwhelmed.

Key needs from our users:

  • Know fast if anything needs her, and by when.
  • Trust what she’s told.
  • Act in one tap.

Assumptions:

  • Fireflies is connected to calendar, Slack and email, and can post for her once she confirms.
  • She has under 90 seconds and one free hand.
  • A normal return has two to five real asks.

What gets surfaced depends on priority and on the user’s role on the team, since this is a context product. We want the user to know where every detail comes from, but if there are 20 Slack messages we can’t throw them all in their face; that would defeat the “without overwhelming the user” part above. So we group and summarise. But grouping Slack notifications on their own would only give us a “Slack round-up”. So how do we group by context instead?

Say we’re working with an engineer. We can take the conversations about a feature, over email and over Slack, add the repo activity on GitHub, and cross-reference all three into a single overview of that work.

Side note:

I’m building an open-source email client and this is one of its features, so I’d already done a lot of the research and ideation. I’d love to chat more about that later.

Approach and key decisions

One hand, 30 to 90 seconds.

The goal was to give the user their content and let them move through the whole app productively with one hand, because this is a 30 to 90 second window of use.

The overview

The first thing on screen is one sentence that answers the whole question: what needs you, what moved and what can wait. Each number can be tapped to jump to that part of the list, and right under it are the sources it was read from, so the summary never floats on its own.

Who is waiting

Each ask leads with the person waiting on it, not the app it came from. A small badge says what they are doing (waiting on you, expecting something from you, or asking you something), so the user knows the shape of the reply before reading a word.

Quick actions

Every ask can be handled without opening it. Native iOS behaviour like the swipe was a quick and easy way to get there: swipe a card left for Later or Done, the same gesture as in Mail and Messages, so the thumb never leaves the bottom half of the screen. Tap the card instead and the suggested next step opens in a sheet, ready to send.

Undo, always

Nothing done with one hand should be permanent. Every swipe, send and snooze comes back with an undo, so moving fast never costs anything.

Later, on your terms

Snoozing asks when, not just whether. Remind me offers three times that fit the day, each with the actual time next to it, so there’s no maths in the hallway. Not mine sits right beside it and teaches Fireflies to weigh asks like that lower next time.

Where it came from

Something small with a large impact is the logos. They reinforce trust in where things are coming from and where they are going, instead of relying on “they should know”. Tap into any ask and the evidence is right there, quoted, with who said it and when.

Where it goes

Throughout the app there are cues telling the user exactly what is going to happen, like confirming that yes, this reply goes to this specific Slack thread. The one-handed feel holds because the AI does most of the typing, drafting each reply for its context.

What can wait

What doesn’t need the user isn’t silently dropped. Projects that moved get one line each with their status, and everything else folds into a single row that says exactly why it was left out.

Ask

Ask sits right where the thumb already is, with suggested questions, so most of the time there is nothing to type.

Up next

There is quick access to calls too, so the user can get some quick prep in, or even join the call on the go and switch devices once they’re settled.

Again, picture this person walking out of a conference room with files or a laptop in hand, trying to figure out “OK, what’s next, and what did I miss?” while already on the way to the next thing. We make sure no unknowns slip past them.
