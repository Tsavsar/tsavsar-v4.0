Fireflies.ai Design Task

by Shater Tsavsar2026

The task was a mobile-first catch-up: open it after a few hours away and know, at a glance, what to do next, without being overwhelmed.

What the user needs:

  • Know fast if anything needs her, and by when.
  • Trust what she’s told.
  • Act in one tap.

Assumptions:

  • Fireflies is connected to calendar, Slack and email, and can post for her once she confirms.
  • She has under 90 seconds and one free hand.
  • A normal return has two to five real asks.

Approach and key decisions

One question, one focus, everything else quiet.

My first version answered every question at once: what needs you, what moved, what can wait, where each thing came from, and who was waiting. All of it was useful, and all of it competed for attention. This version is built on one rule: the screen answers one question, puts one thing in focus, and keeps the rest one tap away.

Answer one question

The first thing on screen is a greeting, how long you’ve been away, and how many things need you, with how many of them are urgent. Nothing else. It’s the question you have walking out of a meeting, and the answer fits in a glance.

One thing in focus

The most urgent ask is the only large thing on the screen: who’s asking on the left, where it came from on the right, then what they need. The one action that resolves it sits just under the card, with the time left on the button. The other asks drop to a quiet list: a face, a title and a time. A one-word tag on each (Urgent, Today or No rush) says how soon, in words rather than colour alone. When you finish the focused ask, the next one takes its place, and the headline counts down with you.

What I took off the list

Most of what made the first version feel busy was true and useful, just in the wrong place. Nothing was thrown away; each piece moved to where it’s needed.

Proof, one tap deep

Trust still matters, so every ask can show where it came from: the meeting, the Slack thread or the email, quoted, with who said it and when. It sits behind one line instead of on the list, so it’s there when you want to check and invisible when you don’t.

Where it goes

Before anything is sent, the sheet says exactly where it’s going: this reply, as you, in Priya’s thread. The AI drafts it for the context, so one hand and one tap is enough.

Fast, and always undoable

Every ask can be handled from the list: swipe left for Later or Done, the same gesture as Mail. Inside an ask, there’s one main button and one quiet “Not now”, which offers real times instead of vague ones. Every action comes back with an undo, so moving fast never costs anything.

What can wait, waits

Projects that moved and updates that don’t need you aren’t hidden, they’re folded: two quiet lines under the asks, each opening in place. The rule for what’s left out is written inside, never implied.

The next meeting, quietly

The next call is a small pill at the bottom: its name and how soon. Tap it for a short prep (the asks to clear before you join and three things worth knowing) and join from there.

Picture someone walking out of a conference room, laptop in hand, already heading to the next thing and asking, “OK, what did I miss?” The answer should take one glance, and acting on it one tap.
